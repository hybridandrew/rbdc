📖 Backstory

Larry was one of three wolves chained in the Cragmaw Cave, likely trained by the goblins as guard animals. When Semaj approached with an incredible animal handling roll, the smallest wolf responded—and a bond was formed.

Fed goblin meat (gross but effective), Larry quickly proved his worth. In the battle against Klarg the bugbear, Larry delivered the killing blow with a natural 20, ripping out the bugbear 's throat in a moment of furry vengeance.

He has since become the party's unofficial seventh member, providing tracking, combat support, and unconditional loyalty. Good boy, Larry. Good boy.

🧠 Psychological Profile

Larry was the smallest of the three wolves chained in Cragmaw Cave, his ribs visible beneath a patchy coat, eating goblin meat out of desperation when Semaj approached with food and patience and a natural twenty on Animal Handling. He pressed his nose into Semaj's fingers. He allowed the chain to be removed and the rope tied. He killed Klarg the bugbear that had chained him — a natural twenty, throat-rip, definitive — and has not looked back. His backstory is the campaign's simplest and most complete arc. He was hungry and cold and someone was kind to him and did not throw rocks, and now he is here. He bites things that threaten his people. He is a very good boy.

What makes Larry remarkable as a character is the precision of his testimony. When Reidoth spoke with him in the Thundertree cottage and Aster translated, Larry reported that the group had dragged him out of trouble, fed him, kept him close instead of chasing him off as most humans do. That they don't run when there is a fight. That they all stay close. That he trusts them. He is a wolf. His account is not complicated by social performance or self-interest or the need to manage impressions. He just said what is true. Reidoth looked at the group differently after that, and so do we. No party member had offered a cleaner character reference for the rest of them.

He dove into the bushes with Semaj during the Venomfang fight — same cover, without being told, without hesitation. He came back from the twig blight encounter with a mouthful of splinters and a wagging tail, waiting to be told he could spit them out, looking at Semaj for confirmation that he had done a good job. He had. He always has. He is not complicated. He is exactly what he appears to be. In this party, that makes him singular.
