📖 Backstory

Standing eight feet tall with the head of a hippopotamus, Aster is impossible to miss. A Giff from the Astral Sea, he found himself on the Material Plane through circumstances he describes as "cosmically hilarious."

Unlike most of his kind, Aster rejected the martial traditions of the Giff in favor of druidic magic, reading meaning in the constellations and drawing power from starlight. He still carries muskets—old habits die hard—but he's just as likely to summon vines or call lightning.

Semaj immediately recognized his astral heritage, and the two share a bond born of the stars.

🧠 Psychological Profile

Aster rejected the martial traditions of the Giff — those enormous, hippopotamus-headed warriors of the Astral Sea, built for exactly the kind of organized military violence he has no interest in — and became instead a druid who reads the stars and carries muskets out of habit. The tension between those two inheritances is not something he has resolved and not something he appears to want to resolve. He carries both because both are accurate. He is eight feet tall with a head like a hippopotamus and he will stop on the road to Thundertree to carefully harvest enough herbs for a healing potion, reduce a mixture over a camp pot with focused patience, and bottle it. The Giff body and the druid's attention to the living world are not in conflict. They are both expressions of the same thing: he is interested in what things are made of.

The apple is the best small evidence of this. He regards one with the concentration of a researcher. He harvested roadside herbs with the focus of a field surgeon. When Kaelia cast Dragon's Breath on Belden in session eleven he complained loudly that she was stealing his kills — which is not the complaint of someone detached from outcomes. He is paying close attention. He used Detect Magic in Glasstaff's workshop to reveal wards and an illusory seam before anyone else attempted anything, and then tried Speak With Animals on the invisible creature in the corner before the party considered combat. When it felt "like talking to a reflection" he reported this accurately and moved on. He finds the closest available framework, tests it, and updates. He is not a lateral thinker under pressure, but he is a thorough one.

The Crail's Maggot deserves full treatment. In the middle of the Venomfang fight — Valrex paralyzed, the party burning through resources — Aster summoned a conjured larva inside the dragon's chest cavity and scored a 23 on the reroll. Alex narrated the death as a grotesque lump sliding beneath the scales, the roar that cut off mid-note. When the dragon hit the ground, Aster walked to the body, reached inside the cavity, felt around until he located the maggot, and ate it. When asked why, the answer implied was: it came from a dragon, it was available, it was interesting, and he had put it there. This is not impulsivity. A man who brews healing potions over a campfire with patience and attention is not impulsive. It is the natural endpoint of sustained curiosity applied to a thing he had a hand in creating. Aster finishes what he starts.

Advantage on STR checks and saves. Counts as one size larger for carrying capacity and grappling.
