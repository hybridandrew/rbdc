📖 Backstory

Beldan was found as an infant by a clan of Hill Giants, who raised him as one of their own. Growing up among creatures four times his size gave him an unusual perspective—and an unusual fighting style.

His beard is unusually short for a dwarf, a habit from his giant upbringing that causes tension whenever he meets his own kind. They see it as disrespectful; he sees it as practical.

Now he wanders the world, seeking to understand where he truly belongs. His warhammer speaks for him when words fail—which is often.

🧠 Psychological Profile

Beldan was raised by creatures four times his size and became, as a result, a creature who fundamentally disagrees with the premise of scale. The horn is not an affectation. It is a philosophy. A dwarf raised by hill giants does not learn that the loudest thing in a room should be the biggest — he learns that the loudest thing in the room sets the terms of the room, and that this is available to anyone with sufficient conviction. His short beard irritates other dwarves for the same reason: he never internalized the conventions that were supposed to shape him. He encountered them too late, already formed. The friction is not defiance. It is just mismatch.

The Thundertree sessions are the best evidence for the gap between how Beldan presents and what Beldan can actually do. He blew the horn on the approach to dragon-occupied ruins, drawing fire from a druid who knew how Venomfang thought. He blew it again in the first ruined cottage and triggered the twig blight ambush. And then, in that ambush, when a blight was in front of him, he stuffed it into the open bell of the horn and blew — a natural 20 critical, twigs and splinters scattering across the combat — and earned inspiration in the same moment. The impulse that created the problem became, on contact with the problem, the solution. This is not luck. It is a worldview being vindicated in real time.

What is less visible but consistently present is the degree to which Beldan takes the party seriously on his own terms. He stood in the middle of the road during the Venomfang confrontation with the horn raised and his chest out, drawing the dragon's full attention while the rest of the party got into cover. He absorbed the poison breath and took nine damage instead of thirty-eight. He never described this as a sacrifice. He experienced it as the obvious thing. The natural 20 investigation in the Thundertree cottage — finding the hidden merchant chest while blowing the horn, the vibrations off the flagstones revealing the seam — is almost too perfect: doing two things at once, the combination working because both were characteristically Beldan. The dragon horn claimed from Venomfang's body before the fight was formally concluded tells you the rest. He already knows what he is building toward.
