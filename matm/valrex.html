📖 Backstory

Valrex bears scars both visible and hidden. Rescued from the Cragmaw goblin caves alongside Sildar Hallwinter, he speaks little of his past—only that he serves a god of war and justice.

His fighting style is brutal and efficient, a holy warrior who believes that evil must be met with overwhelming force. The divine light he calls upon burns with righteous fury.

Since joining the party, he has proven himself time and again, standing firm when others fall and calling down divine judgment on those who threaten the innocent.

🧠 Psychological Profile

Valrex arrived as a prisoner in a cave with nothing but scars and a war god's favor, and his first instinct on being freed was to offer to help skin the snake Larry had just decapitated. This is the throughline of the character: he orients immediately toward what is useful, what needs doing, what the group requires in the next five feet. He speaks little of his past — only that he serves a god of war and justice — but the transcript behavior fills the gap. He is someone who knows what a group looks like from the outside when it has no one willing to hold a difficult position, and he is constitutionally unable to be that gap.

The campaign keeps testing him through a specific form of frustration: the gap between what the War Priest is supposed to do and what the dice allow. He failed every Inflict Wounds attack in the spider encounter. He crit-failed into self-damage. He ended the Venomfang fight paralyzed on the ground, poisoned, having never seen the dragon. He noted this clearly and showed up the next session. This is not stoicism exactly — he makes the observations, he registers the cost — but there is something beneath the frustration that stays solid. He used Channel Divinity to recover spell slots mid-fight without announcement. He Decelerated poison off Semaj. He held the spider-infested building alone for multiple rounds while the rest of the party was still outside, absorbing hits, deploying his spiritual weapon, connecting with nothing in melee, and not leaving. The principle is simple: you are here until the thing is done.

His best moment in the campaign was not combat. It was reading the Black Spider letter in session thirteen, rolling a History check in Dwarvish, and connecting Iarno Albrek to Sildar's missing contact — breaking the central mystery of the Redbrand arc with careful scholarship rather than violence. He delivered the session fourteen recap from memory and Alex called it the best any player had ever managed. Before the Reidoth persuasion check he added his own in-character argument: "You could fall from illness tomorrow, and everything you're protecting is for naught anyway." This from a man who spent a session paralyzed on the floor having never seen the dragon. He has earned the right to make that argument. His faith in the group's capacity to end things is not optimism. It is operational assessment.
