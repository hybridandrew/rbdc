The Domain

The cursed geography of Barovia

What Is Barovia?

Barovia is not a country. It is a prison shaped like one. The Mists of Ravenloft — tendrils of the Ethereal Plane made physical and purposeful — encircle it on all sides and above. No road out of Barovia leads anywhere but back into Barovia. The sun rises here, technically, but it does not warm anything. The sky is permanently overcast. The fog rolls in at night and does not entirely leave in the morning.

The land is medieval Eastern European in character — dark forests, stone villages, mountain roads, a castle on the highest peak. The architecture is old and well-built and suffering. The villagers are human, mostly, and they know what they are living under. They light candles against it. They bolt their shutters. They have learned not to look too carefully at anything after dark and not to name the thing that owns them where it might hear.

Strahd von Zarovich owns it. He has owned it for centuries. He is a vampire lord and a dark wizard and, in the way that matters most, simply a very old and very bored man who has turned an entire domain into an expression of his interiority. He does not want to be defeated. He wants to be interested. The party's arrival is, from his perspective, the most promising thing to happen in some time.

The Module: Curse of Strahd

Curse of Strahd is Dungeons & Dragons' definitive gothic horror module — a sandbox set within the closed domain of Barovia, structured around the Tarokka reading at Tser Pool that randomizes where the module's key magical artifacts are hidden, what form Strahd's allies take, and where he will make his final stand. No two runs of the module play out identically.

The party's objective, on paper, is to defeat Strahd and break the curse that has sealed Barovia from the rest of the multiverse. The module's actual objective is to make them feel what the villagers feel: watched, hunted, and very aware of how much territory Strahd controls versus how much they do.

Survival is the first lesson. The second is that you cannot fight your way through this one. Barovia has to be understood before it can be escaped.

How the Party Got Here

Travelers pulled into the mists don't remember making the choice to enter them. One moment the road was the road they knew. The next, the trees were different — older, darker, spacing wrong — and the fog was behind them as well as ahead. Barovia does not have an entrance you can refuse.

The stakes are immediate and unambiguous: defeat Strahd von Zarovich, the vampire lord who has sealed this domain for centuries, and earn the right to leave. Or die trying. Or die without trying, which is simply slower.

What Barovia does to people is the actual question the campaign will answer. The fog doesn't just trap the body. It works on the mind, on hope, on the assumptions a person carried into it about how the world operates. The party will learn what they're made of by learning what Barovia is willing to do to find out.
