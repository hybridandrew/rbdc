Korrin Lefstryde

Bugbear · Monk 1 / Druid 1 (Primal Order: Magician)

"The flower does not apologize for its thorns."

📖 Backstory

Korrin Lefstryde was not supposed to be gentle. Bugbears are not gentle. The clan that raised him expected him to grow into another raider, another shadow-hunter, another creature of ambush and appetite. What they got instead was a foundling taken in by a druid circle before he could learn to be anything else.

The druids who raised Korrin saw no contradiction in teaching a bugbear to listen — to the roots beneath stone, to the slow grammar of seasons, to the way a forest breathes differently in grief than in growth. He learned to press flowers between pages. He learned to write haiku, badly at first and then with a strange quiet precision. He learned that the impulse to destroy something beautiful because you could is exactly the impulse you needed to understand before you could resist it.

When he outgrew what the circle could teach him — not in knowledge but in the direction of that knowledge — they sent him to a monastery. Not to make him less bugbear. To give the thing already inside him somewhere to go. The monks were confused by him for approximately one week, then they put him to work. A bugbear in a gi at the training post hits much harder than a human, and is much harder to use as an excuse not to train yourself.

Barovia did not ask his permission. The mists came, as they always do, without announcement. He had his quarterstaff and his journal and the Ring of Spell Storing and the certainty that whatever was on the other side of the fog, he would rather face it present and observant than frightened and swinging.

🧠 The Contradiction

The most important thing to understand about Korrin is the gap between how he looks and what he is. He is enormous — a massive upright bugbear with a broad-jawed face, heavy brow ridges, shaggy dark brown fur, and the general silhouette of something that should be alarming. In Barovia, where everyone already lives in low-grade terror, he lands like a physiological warning. People flinch. He is used to this.

What they do not expect is that he will sit down cross-legged and open a journal full of pressed flowers and ask, in a voice that is thoughtful rather than threatening, what kind of grief this place carries. He is not performing gentleness. He earned it, slowly, against his own grain, and he holds it the way you hold something expensive that you paid for yourself.

His golden-amber eyes are the tell. They are warm, curious, and soft-focused in a way that sits strange on a face like his. They are the eyes of someone who is actually paying attention. In this campaign, in this land where most of the NPCs have learned not to see anything too clearly, that quality is either going to save the party or get Korrin into a great deal of trouble by understanding things he was not supposed to understand.

✦ Features & Traits

Bugbear: Surprise Attack
If Korrin surprises a creature and hits it with an attack on the first round of combat, the attack deals an extra 2d6 damage. The nature of a creature raised by druids to value stillness — who nonetheless carries this in his blood.
Bugbear: Long-Limbed
When making a melee attack, Korrin's reach is 5 feet greater than normal. He does not need to close distance the way smaller creatures do.
Unarmored Defense (Monk)
AC equals 10 + DEX modifier + WIS modifier when not wearing armor. The body becomes the armor. The monastery had opinions about this.
Martial Arts
Unarmed strikes and monk weapons use DEX or STR (whichever is higher). Can make one unarmed strike as a bonus action after attacking with a monk weapon or unarmed strike.
Primal Order: Magician
Korrin adds his WIS modifier to his Arcana checks. He can also use INT as the ability for his druid spells instead of WIS — reflecting his scholarly, studious approach to magic.
Druidic & Thieves' Cant
Knows the secret language of druids and can understand hidden messages in natural settings. The journal he keeps is partly in druidic script — not for secrecy, but habit.
Ring of Spell Storing
A magic ring that can store up to 5 levels of spells cast into it, to be released by the wearer at a later time. A gift from the druid circle when he left. They wanted him to have something they'd already prepared for him.
